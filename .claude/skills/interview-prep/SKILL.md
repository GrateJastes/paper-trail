# interview-prep

The output is **fuel for riffing, not a script**. Questions asked off the
interviewer's own words land better than rehearsed ones, and an over-scripted
candidate sounds like one — the prep doc exists so the material is loaded, not so
it gets recited.

## Inputs

Read, in this order: `NOTES.md` (especially the process log, screening-call facts,
and tailoring decisions), `jd.md`, the **sent** CV (that exact one — it's what the
interviewers have in front of them), and the general variant for material that was
cut but might come up.

## Steps

1. Write to `prep/<round>-prep.md` inside the application folder (e.g.
   `prep/tech-round-prep.md`). Prep docs are living — update them as new intel
   arrives.

2. **Read the room first.** What is this round actually deciding, given
   everything in NOTES.md? Who is in it, and what does each person need to see?
   If the candidate has names, suggest they look the interviewers up — but only
   facts actually gathered go in the doc. Never invent a "read" on a person.

3. **Story bank.** Map the JD's themes to 3–5 real stories from the CV and notes
   — each with the situation, the action, the number, and what it proves. Only
   stories grounded in the CV or confirmed by the candidate: the truthfulness
   gate applies in the interview room too.

4. **Name the gaps first.** List the requirements the candidate genuinely doesn't
   meet, and for each, an honest way to hold it: named proactively, paired with
   the nearest real experience and a concrete learning step. Gaps said first land
   better than gaps discovered.

5. **The openers.** A ~90-second "tell me about yourself" sketch (beats, not a
   monologue) and honest answers to the two questions every process asks: "why
   are you leaving?" and "why us?".

6. **Questions to bring** — as themes to riff on, not a fixed list. Enough of
   them (8–10 themes) that some survive being answered during the conversation.

7. **Logistics** at the top: date, time, platform, who. If the company uses an AI
   notetaker, the move is to allow it and ask for a copy of the recording for
   self-review — never to opt out.

8. Afterwards, debrief into NOTES.md: what was asked, what landed, what didn't,
   corrections to Role facts. The debrief is worth more than the prep — it's what
   the next round's prep reads.
